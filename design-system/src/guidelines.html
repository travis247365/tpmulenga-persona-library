01 · Overview

The system in one page.

TPCL runs a two-mode system. Charcoal and white do all the work. Dark mode carries openers, section breaks and statements. Light mode carries content, data and bios. Everything else — the accents, the motifs, the type — hangs off that one decision.

ModeCarriesTypeAccent
Charcoal #3A3A3ACovers, breaks, statements Bold, ALL CAPSCyan
White #FFFFFFContent, data, bios Regular, sentence caseAmber

The case rule is the system. Caps on charcoal signals a break; sentence case on white signals substance. Do not enforce one case across a deck.

Non-negotiables

  • Poppins only — Regular 400, Medium 500, Bold 700. Nothing else.
  • Pure white #FFFFFF. Never off-white.
  • Radius 0. No shadows, no gradients, no glassmorphism. Depth comes from tone, never elevation.
  • Where a dark and a light block meet in a split, they meet directly — there is no divider line.
  • Cyan and amber fills always carry charcoal labels, never white.
  • Every figure states its provenance: Measured · Modelled · Assumed.
  • Every total reconciles to source, or the work is not finished.

02 · Identity

The mark, and the four ways it may appear.

Full colour
Light surfaces with no competing colour. Covers, letterheads, footers.
Black
The default on white. Any surface already carrying an accent colour.
Tint · #57585A
Watermarks, document grounds, partner walls. Never below 40%.
Reversed white
Charcoal and black surfaces. The deck-cover default.

Clear space

45% of the logo height on all four sides — equivalently 23.5% of its width. The padding is uniform, not proportional to the side. Nothing enters it: no text, no rule, no accent block, no photo edge.

Measured from the 2021 master travis paul_logo_Clear Space.ai (bbox 232 × 121, padding 54–55px). This is roughly double what most people leave by eye.

Placement, sizing and co-branding

  • Dark surface → bottom LEFT. Light surface → bottom RIGHT — diagonally opposite the top-left accent block. That diagonal is the TPCL page frame.
  • Ratio locked at 1.93:1. Never stretch, condense, outline or shadow it.
  • Minimum: 120px wide on screen, 34px tall in a slide footer, 28mm wide in print.
  • Never on cyan or amber, never on a mid-tone below 4.5:1, never straddling two tones or sitting on a busy photograph.
  • Co-branding: TPCL left, client right, 1px rule between, heights matched optically not by bounding box. On a client's own document the client mark leads and TPCL is never the larger of the two.
  • Consulting is the default on all client-facing work. Holdings appears only on parent-entity material.

The ruling

The logo is a fixed asset and keeps its own colours — never recolour it to match the palette. The layout keeps charcoal and system cyan. But because #59D1E2 and #00BCD4 are close without being equal, never place the full-colour logo on a surface that also carries a cyan accent block. The near-miss reads as a production error rather than a choice. Use the black or reversed-white cut there instead — which is why every template in this system uses black-on-white and white-on-charcoal, and full colour appears only on plain grounds.

The five rules that follow

  • Cyan and amber fills take charcoal labels, never white. White on cyan is 2.30:1 and fails.
  • On white, cyan and amber are graphic-only — blocks, rules, dots, borders, fills behind dark text. Never body copy, never a link colour.
  • On charcoal both are safe as text. This is why the cyan timeline and cyan hero figures live on dark surfaces.
  • Blue-gray is a surface — 1.75:1 on white. It can never be read as text on white.
  • One accent per surface. Cyan and amber do not share a page, except where amber is the accent block and cyan is a data mark.

Client overlay: engagement work tints exactly one variable, --tp-client, and swaps the co-brand logo. Frame, type and motifs stay TPCL. Check any client colour against charcoal and white before using it as text.

06 · Motifs

The brand DNA

Five repeated elements. They are what make a page read as TPCL when the logo is small and the colours are borrowed from a client. Use two or three per surface — never all five.

01 · Accent block

Upper-left corner, ~2.8:1 ratio. Amber on white, cyan on charcoal. On every internal page and absent from covers — that absence is what makes a cover a cover. Placed first; everything aligns to its margin.

07

02 · Index chip

34px blue-gray square, white numeral, flush to the right edge, vertically centred. It bleeds off the edge; it is never inset. On a blue-gray panel it inverts to charcoal.

03 · Outline rectangles

2px cyan stroke, no fill. Texture in the empty half of a dark page, or a frame around a statement. Keep them unequal, unaligned and bleeding off the margin. On light pages the stroke turns charcoal.

04 · Cyan timeline

2px vertical rule, 12px round dots each ringed by a 4px halo in the background colour. The standard treatment for anything sequential. Every item carries a time label — a timeline without dates is just a list.

1 2 3

05 · Step boxes

40px squares, 2px cyan stroke, bold numeral — where a timeline would be too heavy. Square, because the circle is reserved for the timeline dot.

Two motifs minimum.
Three maximum.

Combination rules

The signature sits diagonally opposite the accent block. Outline rectangles and the timeline never share a page — both are cyan stroke work and they compete. And in a split layout there is no divider line.

07 · Layout

An 8px scale, a 12-column grid, and square corners everywhere.

Spacing & grid

  • Scale in multiples of 8: 4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 48 · 64 · 96 · 128.
  • Heading→body 16 · block→block 32 · section→section 64.
  • 12 columns, 24px gutter, 1200px content in a 1440px frame.
  • Prefer 7/5 and 5/7 splits, not 6/6. Half-and-half is reserved for the dark/light mode split, where the colour supplies the asymmetry.
  • Radius is 0. Every card, chip, button, crop and accent block is square. The only round things are the timeline dot and, where a UI needs one, a pill filter.
  • Strokes: 1px hairline · 2px outline · 3px accent rule. No shadows, no gradients.

Data & evidence

  • Every figure carries provenance — Measured, Modelled or Assumed. A modelled number is never shown as measured. This rule overrides visual preference: if the chip crowds the layout, change the layout.
  • Tables: no vertical rules, no zebra stripes, no fills. One 2px charcoal rule under the header, hairlines between rows.
  • Numbers right-aligned and tabular; text left-aligned; never centre a column.
  • Totals bold, unfilled, and they must reconcile to source.
  • KPI rows: four tiles maximum. Deltas name the comparison ("vs June"), never a bare percentage.

08 · Before it ships

Nine checks

  1. Two to three motifs — not one, not five.
  2. Accent block top-left; signature diagonally opposite.
  3. Case rule respected: CAPS on charcoal, sentence case on white.
  4. No divider line where a dark and light block meet.
  5. Charcoal labels on every cyan or amber fill.
  1. Square corners, no shadows, no gradients.
  2. Logo clear space at 45% of its height, unbroken.
  3. Every figure tagged Measured, Modelled or Assumed.
  4. Every total reconciles to source.

The last two are not design rules. They are the reason the design exists — TPCL sells evidence, and a modelled number presented as measured is the one reputational mistake that cannot be walked back.
